What BSTVC helps you examine

BSTVC Desktop is a graphical environment for Bayesian analysis of relationships that vary across space and time. It combines local coefficients, global spatiotemporal contribution, and prediction within a common workflow. Standard desktop model fitting does not require R programming; R is optional for customized figures.

Research taskQuestionOutputs
Local interpretationHow do associations vary by location or period?Spatial coefficients (SCs), temporal coefficients (TCs), credible intervals
Global contributionWhich modeled components contribute to spatiotemporal variation?Spatiotemporal Variance Partitioning Index (STVPI)
Dynamic predictionWhat values does the model estimate for observed or missing outcomes?Predictions, smoothing, imputation, and uncertainty
Contribution is not causal attribution.

These models describe conditional associations and model-based contributions. They may provide important prior evidence for causal research, but do not establish causal effects on their own.

The methodological framework

BSTVC stands for Bayesian Spatiotemporally Varying Coefficients. The model represents relationships that can change across locations and periods within one Bayesian hierarchical framework. The source manual calls this a whole-map framework: local estimates are fitted together, with spatial and temporal structure, rather than obtained by fitting an unrelated regression separately to every location.

Two ideas guide the analysis. Autocorrelation represents dependence among nearby places or neighboring periods. Nonstationarity allows the relationship between a predictor and the response to differ across those places or periods. Accounting for dependence and estimating variation in relationships answer different questions; a spatially patterned outcome alone does not demonstrate a spatially varying predictor effect.

ApproachScopePractical distinction
BSTVCRepeated observations across space and timeUse a location–time panel and interpret temporal and spatial coefficient outputs.
BSVCSpatial observations from one cross-sectionUse the spatial modeling module for a single time point; repeated years should not be treated as independent map units.
STVPIContribution based on the fitted modelSummarize relative spatial, temporal, and combined contributions, with posterior uncertainty.

The methods cited in the Chinese manual discuss the STVI, STIVI, STVC, and STIVC model family. These are methodological variants, not interchangeable labels for the three response types in this tutorial. The walkthroughs use the documented desktop controls; consult the method references before selecting or extending a statistical specification.

The framework can support interpretation, smoothing, and prediction, including model-based estimates for missing outcomes. Whether it improves prediction or yields useful evidence depends on the data, model assumptions, and validation. Posterior uncertainty describes uncertainty under the fitted model; it does not remove bias from incorrect data alignment, confounding, or an unsuitable neighborhood.

For methodological details and programmable extensions, use BSTVC-R. To read the desktop outputs, continue to Understand results.

About this guide

This English edition consolidates the supplied July 2026 desktop manual with the fuller Chinese online manual, reorganizes their workflows, and checks case filenames, identifiers, dimensions, and missingness against the accompanying data. It targets the English Windows desktop release v2026.8.1. Interface illustrations originate from the supplied manual; historical figures illustrate how to read outputs and are not a new reproduction of model fitting.

The spatiotemporal interpretability research organization and BSTVC development team maintains the BSTVC ecosystem. For programmable workflows, see BSTVC-R. Guide revised: 2 October 2026.
